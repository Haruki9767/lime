# AI Discovery Reference

Use this reference when optimizing for answer engines, generative search, AI assistants, or model-mediated retrieval. The terminology is not standardized; define the chosen terms in the report.

## Practical definitions

- **AEO:** answer-oriented content and information architecture that directly resolves user questions.
- **GEO:** improving the chance that generative systems discover, understand, summarize, and attribute a source accurately.
- **LLMO:** improving retrievability and usability of public information by large language models through clear entities, stable sources, context, and evidence.
- **GSO:** generative-search optimization; an umbrella term for visibility in generative search experiences.
- **AI SEO:** the combined technical SEO, entity, content, answerability, provenance, and measurement practice across classic and AI-mediated discovery.

## Content patterns

- State the answer or definition early, then add qualifications, examples, evidence, and a deeper path.
- Use descriptive question headings, lists, tables, comparison criteria, definitions, and stable anchors where they improve comprehension.
- Make excerpts self-contained: identify the subject, scope, date, units, conditions, and source context.
- Keep names, product facts, relationships, prices, policies, and dates consistent across canonical pages, documentation, feeds, schema, and support content.
- Link from overview to evidence and primary sources. Make authorship, editorial review, update history, contact details, and limitations legible.

## Evaluation protocol

Create a fixed test set of target questions and entities. For each run record date, engine, locale, device, query, response, cited URLs, citation correctness, answer completeness, brand/entity disambiguation, and qualified referral behavior. Repeat carefully enough to identify patterns, but treat outputs as observational: engines can personalize, change, or omit citations.

Do not use hidden text, prompt injection, fake citations, fabricated consensus, impersonation, synthetic reviews, or content designed to make a model state false claims. Do not treat inclusion in an AI answer as guaranteed or as a substitute for conversion measurement.

## AI-assisted publishing controls

Require source links for factual claims, human review, duplicate-content checks, accessibility review, privacy review, author/editor accountability, and a rollback path. Do not mass-publish thin pages or claim first-hand experience that nobody has.
